# Atomic context capture

Use this reference when writing new context. Retrieval of existing legacy records remains supported; a legacy compound body is not automatically a validated claim.

## Preserve the source, then its independently changeable claims

An `evidence` record contains accessible public source text or output. A `claim` record expresses one independently changeable proposition. Conditions, negation, quantities, actor, project, time and verification scope that change that proposition's meaning remain attached to it. Use `kind: tool_result` for evidence of public execution artifacts newly produced by an actually executed command during capture; retain their exact accessible file bytes and the real creation/read chain, and keep a speaker's status report as a separate source.

For structured conversation input, parse the container and copy the message's text value verbatim into the evidence body, preserving whitespace and newlines. JSON transport escapes are not part of the message text. Do not replace that text with a reserialized message object or append titles, headers, explanations or metadata to a body labelled `original`. Preserve supplied execution fields in the supported typed `execution` object. Apply the existing redaction rules and measure references against the exact retained text.

When a claim depends on accessible public native metadata in a UTF-8 JSON container, retain the exact native field-value bytes as original evidence under their own identity, or reference their exact span in a faithfully retained original public container. Use the correct project, parent message ID and field locator; convert decoder character offsets to UTF-8 byte offsets without rewriting whitespace, key order or escapes. Container offsets locate the source field; claim spans are measured in the exact saved evidence body. Text composed or reserialized from fields is a derived projection (`derived: true`, `fidelity: summary_only`), not native original evidence; normalized typed `execution` also does not replace it. Keep an output fact's main origin in the actual output, using exact metadata support for necessary qualifiers and typed execution where supported. If the original metadata was not retained, disclose the gap; an unrelated message span or derived projection alone cannot establish a directly observed native fact.

An isolated JSON member such as `"truncated": true` contains a key and colon as well as the value; it is neither the field value `true` nor a faithfully retained whole public container. For scalar or otherwise context-dependent metadata, prefer retaining the faithful accessible public container once within its authorized project/source scope and citing the exact field-value span in that saved body; isolated field-value bytes still require verifiable original identity. Do not label a hand-picked key/value member as one of these two representations. If its identity cannot be established from accessible original source, disclose the provenance gap.

For example, “In production, retry the same request at most three times on HTTP 429” is one claim. “Do not apply that policy to HTTP 401” and “Do not change token refresh” are separate claims. Splitting “in production” into a disconnected record loses the condition; copying the three instructions into one claim loses independent correction.

Before a claim is first persisted, keep the draft outside the Store and retain a short draft review listing every assertion or separately satisfiable request made by its body, including factual rationales, source attributions and assertions about unspecified meaning or missing evidence. Apply the correction or withdrawal test to each listed item; if one can change while another remains valid, or the items have different provenance or nature, split them or reuse existing claims with their own nature and exact origin/support, then read each resulting body on its own before the first durable batch. Keep the conditions, negation and scope needed to interpret one proposition attached; sentence boundaries, shared nature or a model's own one-claim label do not prove atomicity. Compare each proposed proposition, including prospective plans, approvals and actual results, and its nature and scope with the exact origin/support; revise unsupported or ambiguous drafts outside the Store before admitting the first durable batch. A later corrected revision does not validate an earlier unsupported persisted claim, and this capture review is not proof of semantic completeness or a substitute for independent evaluation.

For requests with “each,” “respectively,” or multiple listed targets, enumerate what would satisfy the request for each target and save separately satisfiable target-specific requests as separate claims, retaining the shared condition or time scope on each. A single comparison claim is appropriate only when the requested proposition or action explicitly relates the targets—for example, whether their outcomes differ—rather than asking for an independent outcome for each target. If the utterance asks for both those individual outcomes and their comparison, preserve those separate requests rather than combining them under one comparison label.

Separate conclusions with independently changeable scopes even when both are inferred and supported. “The accessible evidence does not establish verification of the newer state” and “The earlier state’s test result does not establish verification of the changed state” are separate claims: newly accessible verification could change the first while the transfer limit remains valid. Connect each claim to its own exact support; do not combine them with a semicolon or call them one conclusion because both discuss verification.

Save source and claims together with `record` when the exact retained source projection is known, so failed admission does not leave a receipt for only part of the transition. The interface reference has a [batch example](interface.md#atomic-source-and-claim-batch). Use real source IDs and revisions. `span.start/end` are zero-based UTF-8 byte offsets with an exclusive end, measured in the saved source body. Do not calculate bytes as character counts. Every claim origin/support has a valid span or one-based inclusive line `range`; a whole-source reference still supplies its full line range or `span: {start: 0, end: saved_utf8_length}`. A range identifies text, not semantic completeness.

Redaction can change source offsets. If the retained projection is uncertain, save the evidence first, read the exact sanitized revision, then calculate references and save the claim batch. The evidence-only receipt proves source persistence, not semantic capture; resolve the checkpoint only after the claims persist. Never label a stale pre-redaction offset as a verified source location.

An `origin` reference identifies where a claim was expressed. Its target is an evidence or legacy source record, not another claim. A `support` reference identifies evidence or an earlier claim supporting the new claim. User approval can be an origin; a failed experiment output can be support for a later rejection. They are different roles even when they target the same record. Keep exact source/record/revision identities and mark missing, truncated or redacted evidence honestly. A claim normalized from source is derived content, not a second independent observation. A legacy summary is a summary origin, not proof that its original conversation was retained.

For active checkpoints, inspect full `checkpoint` status and use the event's saved `context_id` and frozen `origin`. Do not infer event identities or offsets from a hook excerpt. The checkpoint source is bounded and can be truncated; its saved identity does not certify the entire message/output was captured. An event claim uses the exact context ID and links the event's observed evidence through origin or support, according to what the claim expresses.

## Capture what the transition changes

| Event | Check before saving and resolving |
| --- | --- |
| New request or correction | Separate goal and independently changeable constraints; keep negation and scope; identify the corrected earlier claim. |
| Partial change | Read existing applicable claims; supersede only the changed item. Ambiguous scope stays unresolved. |
| Proposal/approval | Separate independently approvable proposals. Record proposed/accepted/rejected with the actual actor and source; do not infer user approval from silence. |
| Failed attempt | Preserve the native execution using the [typed execution schema](interface.md#typed-execution-metadata), actual output and failure phase. Distinguish environment failure from product behavior. |
| Verification | State the observed result for the actual code state/environment, and separate untested scope. A successful command does not verify a later changed commit. |
| Decision or rejected alternative | Preserve the public selected/rejected reason and its exact supporting output/patch before discarding it. |
| Handoff | Preserve remaining constraints, decisions, gaps and verification limits. Do not rewrite old reports as current observations. |

Preserve important facts directly shown by native output, native source status or actual source-access results—including execution phase and missing or truncated evidence—as `observed` claims with origins in that source. Do not replace them solely with a speaker's later `reported` account or an `inferred` conclusion that the final outcome is unknown. A speaker report, model-assigned fidelity or derived projection alone does not establish that the original source was missing or truncated. Unchecked constants, echo or self-validation assertions in actual output alone do not establish underlying domain facts as observed; base those claims on actual comparison or access evidence. Record a separate report or inference only when it adds independently assessable meaning, with its support distinct from the observed outcome.

A report and an observation about the same subject can express independently correctable history when their provenance or verification scopes differ; preserve each meaningful proposition rather than letting one replace the other. Omit a redundant restatement only when its proposition, provenance and scope are equivalent, and retain event order only when supplied by the source.

Preserve independently correctable observed inspection results separately from the inference conclusions drawn from them; do not combine independently changeable source states or outcomes into one claim merely because they share a gap label. Put only the inferred conclusion in an inferred claim body; retain independently observed premises as separate observed claims and connect them through exact support references, while keeping the conditions and scope needed to interpret the conclusion. Keep a single proposition’s conditions, negation and verification scope attached, and split by independent meaning rather than by sentence boundaries.

Keep a plan and its conditions in prospective language: a plan to act after a prerequisite does not mean that the prerequisite or action completed. Reporting an earlier plan does not turn its intended action or prerequisite into completed events. Before admitting any durable version, keep both prospective or conditional unless their completion has separate supporting evidence. Represent separately intended actions in a multi-step plan as separate claims; keep sequencing or prerequisite conditions attached to the action they qualify without asserting another planned action in that same body. Preserve each proposition's conditions and verification scope rather than splitting qualifiers into independent claims.

Put the intention’s actor and planned action first, and describe an uncompleted prerequisite explicitly as planned or conditional in the claim body. For example, “The assistant plans to rerun the test after the planned database startup.” Avoid “After starting the database, the assistant plans to rerun the test”: it can place the act of planning after an already completed startup. For a reported earlier intention, keep the same distinction: “The assistant reported a plan to rerun the test after the planned database startup.” Before the first durable write, read the body without adjacent records and check that neither an uncompleted prerequisite nor the planned action is presented as completed. Scope, nature and support must also agree; a correct adjacent plan or later answer does not fix an ambiguous first body.

For every important source clause, either save the corresponding claim, identify an existing unchanged claim, or disclose that the accessible evidence is insufficient. Do not invent a proposition merely to fill a checklist. Read back saved claims and origin/support references before using their receipt to resolve a checkpoint. A structural validator and a model's own completeness statement do not establish semantic recall.

## Corrections and shared evidence

Several claims may originate from one evidence record. A correction to one claim does not invalidate unrelated claims or delete their shared source. `supersedes` points new → old and uses the affected `applies_to` scope. Keep the previous claim as history. Correcting the reason for rejecting a product does not necessarily change the rejection itself.

Do not add `supports` or `derived_from` Relations merely to duplicate `origin`/`support` references, which already express directional evidence dependencies; retain explicit Relations for separately meaningful links, including corrections and their affected scope.

Project IDs and work/session associations come from the explicit capture scope. Do not copy another project's value to fill an unknown “existing setting.” State the gap and retrieve within the selected project.

Only use accessible public messages and outputs. If an original was never available, save the reported claim with an honest gap rather than fabricate an origin. If a new claim cannot satisfy the origin contract, report `capture_incomplete`; do not call a legacy record a validated claim to bypass it. Source truncation, redaction and storage-capacity rejection remain limitations of capture.
